What an Alternative Investment Fund Is

An AIF is a SEBI-regulated, privately pooled investment vehicle that invests in asset classes outside the reach of a traditional mutual fund - private equity, structured credit, real assets, long-short equity strategies, and more. SEBI organises AIFs into three categories based on strategy and leverage:

CAT I

Category I

Funds investing in start-ups, SMEs, infrastructure, and other sectors considered socially or economically desirable by the government or regulators.

CAT II

Category II

Private equity and debt funds that don't fall under Category I or III, and don't use leverage other than for day-to-day operational needs.

CAT III

Category III

Funds employing complex or diverse trading strategies, including listed derivatives, potentially using leverage - the closest to a traditional hedge fund structure.

Why Jaipur Investors Consider AIFs

AIFs are built for investors who've already established a diversified core portfolio and are looking for genuine diversification into strategies mutual fund regulations simply don't permit. The tradeoff is real: higher minimum investment (SEBI mandates ₹1 crore), longer lock-in periods, and materially less liquidity than a mutual fund or even PMS. AIFs aren't a "better" version of a mutual fund - they're a structurally different tool for a specific kind of investor.

What an AIF Distributor Actually Does

As an AMFI-registered AIF distributor, we help you understand and access AIF strategies from SEBI-registered fund managers - explaining the category, mandate, fee structure, and lock-in terms of each option, and supporting onboarding and ongoing servicing. We're a distributor, not the fund manager - the investment decisions within the fund are made by the AIF's own manager, not by us.

Who May Consider AIF

Eligible, informed investors (SEBI minimum ₹1 crore) seeking exposure beyond conventional mutual funds, comfortable with product complexity, longer lock-in horizons, and limited liquidity.

What to Evaluate Before Choosing

Fund category and strategy, manager experience and track record, lock-in and exit terms, valuation methodology, fee structure, leverage used, portfolio concentration, and tax treatment.
